### Wednesday, 22 October, 3:20pm

How long will it take me to become good at programming? Am I in the valley of despair? I think so... How long before I reach the slope of enlightenment, or the plateau of sustainability. I do genuinely think that if I was good at it I would enjoy it, and that I would like being good at it, but sometimes I start doing it and it does not interest me at all and I feel like perhaps I am wasting my time. I feel that perhaps I enjoy the aesthetic of it more than I enjoy the reality of it, but I don't really know. I think I also hate the feeling that there is so much that I don't know. When I do any programming, even if it is a project that is just for fun, just for myself, I feel that surely I am approaching it wrong, that there are industry best practices I don't know about and that soon I will be revealed to be an incompetent and inept fool. I guess that is not a bad thing — building things is valuable even if you do it poorly. I suppose the only answer is to just keep going. Do it everyday. Improve, slowly. I know that being bad at this nags me. So let me fix that.

### Sunday, 6 October, 11:00pm

Maybe I should operate under the belief that I will be appreciated only after I am dead. This way the focus is only on doing and making work that I think is valuable — let it be appreciated after I am gone. As long as I think it is good then I am doing well. Once I am dead it will be revered.

I live worrying about the significance of the things I do. How do I live a life that matters, a life that doesn't just fade in nothingness once I'm gone, an existence that lingers and impacts others in a positive way? How do I make my name remain, and not be just another faded mark on a gravestone? Perhaps I will live my life with the belief that I will be famous once I'm dead. I'll make art, write, and work 'knowing' that one day everything I am doing will be revered, that this work is for the people. Does this liberate me to create more freely, this knowledge that what I am doing *will* have an impact? For some reason, the idea appeals to me. It sounds like the sort of manic confidence that is needed for great work.
